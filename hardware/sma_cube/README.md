# SMA cube: printable CAD

Generated by `hardware/scripts/module_phys/sma_cube_cad.py` (CadQuery 2.8). The script is parametric in L and reads the design from `hardware/runs/module_phys/sma_cube/chosen.json` by default:

    .venv/bin/python hardware/scripts/module_phys/sma_cube_cad.py                     # chosen.json, writes here
    .venv/bin/python hardware/scripts/module_phys/sma_cube_cad.py --L 14              # override L only
    .venv/bin/python hardware/scripts/module_phys/sma_cube_cad.py --design hardware/runs/module_phys/sma_cube/chosen_14mm_provisional.json --out hardware/sma_cube/l14_provisional
    .venv/bin/python hardware/scripts/module_phys/sma_cube_cad.py --no-export         # checks, flex net and check.json only

A full run takes about 4 minutes on the Mac CPU (the overlap check intersects about 1,500 part pairs).

**Main build: the final chosen point, L = 12.18 mm** (NSGA-II pick: gear joint `geared_joint.nominal_design`, legs 0.817 L on one 76 um Flexinol strand per wire, corner spheres 0.061 L, bolt travel 3.18 mm = 0.6 mm hook travel plus 2.58 mm sphere retraction, LiPo 301010, packing 1.53). The 14 mm provisional point (bolt travel 2.4 mm, LiPo 401012) is a secondary build in `l14_provisional/` with the same file set.

Every number below is from `check.json` (12.18 mm unless marked 14 mm).

## Verdict

The parts are drawn and printable one by one (apart from the at-risk features in the table), but **the cube does not assemble at 12.18 mm, nor at 14 mm**. The overlap check finds 164 intersecting pairs (206 mm3) at 12.18 mm and 159 pairs (173 mm3) at 14 mm. Each item below is a "does not fit", with the amount it misses by:

1. **Geared joints and battery.** The joint stack (disc face down to the Didel worm's underside) needs 6.37 mm of depth along x on each side. Beside the 3.0 mm LiPo slab in the middle there is 4.54 mm. The two joints and the battery overlap by 148 mm3 (31 pairs); the 7.3 mm flywheel rotor alone overlaps the cell by 23 mm3 per side. The rotor slab sits under a plain face (axis y, parallel to the worm arbor), and it overlaps the cell by 1.42 mm in y.
1. **Capstan layer.** The only x layer left for the four leg capstans (r 1.72 mm ring, 54 teeth) is the layer of the split-ring output ring (r up to 4.39 mm). Capstans and output ring overlap by 0.98 mm3 each, and the capstan pawls hit the planets and the ring (about 4 mm3 in total).
1. **Edge-bolt hooks.** With 3.18 mm of travel, a hook slot is 3.78 mm long (shank 0.6 mm plus travel). An edge region between the bayonet groove boss and the corner sphere pocket has at most 3.4 mm, even for a 0.3 mm wide tab. **No hook tab fits at 12.18 mm**, so the bars carry no tabs and the faces have no hook slots. At 14 mm (2.4 mm travel), one tab per bolt face fits at 0.61 mm wide instead of 1.54 mm (40 % of a design tab's strength). The design's 2 tabs a side would need 12.6 mm of slot length against 7.5 mm available.
1. **Bolt bars against the bayonet groove boss.** The groove channel under each plain face (inward lips, boss to r 5.18 mm) runs into the corner bars at 1.98-2.73 mm3 per bar. Under legs XP and XN, the leg recess floor runs into them as well.
1. **Legs against the steel inserts.** The leg strip (half width 2.29 mm with clearance) covers the face-diagonal insert holes by 0.37 mm (0.71 mm3 in total).
1. **Bayonet under the legs.** Each leg strip covers 60 degrees of the groove at each end of its face axis, which leaves a 30 degree window off-axis. A 45 degree bayonet throw (entry on the axes, lock on the diagonals) loses 2 of its 4 entry notches on every plain face. The fix is a 22.5 degree throw, with entry and lock both inside the window. Disc-to-disc bayonets cannot work with the same tab pattern on both discs: the partner's tabs land on the own tab roots. Disc-disc bonds are held by magnets and pins only.
1. **Flex electronics.** No IC fits on the outer net. After the windows (leg strips, bayonet slits and notches, insert keep-outs, corner contacts, pin sockets) and the 26 crimp pads, the largest free patches are fragments of 29-39 mm2 per panel. The MCU (QFN-20 3x3), IMU (2x2), charger (SOT-23-5), 6 tantalum 1206 and all 19 FETs are unplaced. They need an inner flex tail, and there is no free volume for one. At 14 mm, 15 FETs fit and everything else is still unplaced.
1. **Moving parts (swept over the travel, 23 pairs, 5.2 mm3).** Bolts pp and nn sweep through capstans DP and XN (0.91 mm3 each). The bolt noses and the retracting carriers reach into the end-plate posts (0.13-0.34 mm3). The retracting carriers hit the capstans (0.20 mm3 each).

What the check does show working: the carriers and corner-sphere pockets clear the full 2.58 mm retraction against the frame. The bolt's ramp noses stay in contact over the whole retraction (the noses are extended 2.58 mm inward along the bisector; see "Edge bolts"). The disc bayonet tabs, the split-ring gears (meshing pairs are excluded from the count), the legs and their hinge pins all fit their own neighbours.

## Design decisions and deviations from the concept

1. **Joint = `geared_joint.nominal_design`** (Didel G209 on the rotor pin, a G209 idler on the selector arm, a G220 on the worm arbor, the GW205 worm into a G220 on the sun shaft, then a printed split ring: sun 10, planets 8 x3, rings 26/29, module 0.247 at 12.18 mm). The output ring is the disc's hub; the fixed ring is part of the end plate. Because the motor axis is parallel to the worm arbor (perpendicular to x), **the flywheel's stator coils cannot be under the disc.** They sit on a flap of the net folded in under the rotor, which lies under the +y face (+x joint) or the -y face (-x joint). The flaps carry 3 sector coils on 2 layers (`flex_net.svg`, above the +x and -x panels).
1. **Frame = tube plus two end plates.** Internals go in through the open tube ends. The bolts slide in along x, then the carriers drop into the corners.
1. **Bayonet lips point radially inward.** Outward lips put the groove boss at r 6.4 (14 mm) and leave no room for any hook slot. Entry notches are on the face axes and the lock is on the diagonals, after a 45 degree turn.
1. **Hooks per bolt face: 1, not 2**, and only where they fit (14 mm build). At 12.18 mm there are none (see Verdict 3). The tab pattern is hermaphroditic: in each edge region, one own slot at +u and one receive slot at -u along x, with the a/b assignment carried round the four bolts by the 90 degree symmetry about x. Two cubes with parallel x axes latch through either cube's bolt in all 16 face pairings, in both x directions. The slot pattern is symmetric about x = 0, and each slot is shank plus travel long, so a 180 degree turn maps own slots onto receive slots. The faces that carry the hinge edges of XP and XN (the +y edge region next to bolt pp, the -y one next to bolt nn) have no hook slots. Those regions are under the leg and its knuckle, so a +-y face bonds with one bolt instead of two.
1. **Leg hinge axes** sit inset by the knuckle radius from both faces, 0.49 mm off the lattice edge line, so the knuckle stays inside the envelope. On the edge line itself, the 0.35 mm knuckle would stand 0.35 mm proud of both faces. It cannot lie inside a 0.5 mm chamfer: that would need a chamfer of at least 1.2 mm.
1. **Edge bolt, corner carrier drive.** The bar sits in the corner of the two skins. Its ends are 45 degree ramps that meet the carriers' ramp faces after the first 0.6 mm of travel (the hook-freeing part), then push the carriers 2.58 mm inward along the bisector. The bar stays in the corner while the carrier moves away from it, so a nose limited to the bar's section loses contact after about 1.0 mm. The noses are therefore extended 2.58 mm inward along the bisector as ramp blocks. **Only the leading end pushes.** For the trailing (-x) carrier, a rigid bar moving +x needs a pull link (hook or pin-in-slot); this is sketched, not in CAD, and the corner triangle (about 1.2 mm deep) has no room for it. Racks of 0.2 mm square teeth sit on the bar's two inner faces: the drive pawl is under the -y-side rack and the lock pawl beside the -z-side rack.
1. **Pawls** are one generic part. Three per capstan (open, lock, close) fit only with a 0.08 mm3 overlap between neighbours: three 3.8 mm pawls around a 3.4 mm ring do not fit in one 0.9 mm layer. The leg lock pawl is assumed lifted by a cam on each drive pawl's stroke (sketched; the design has no lock wire for legs).
1. **Bolt and corner tabs stand proud.** Hook tabs (14 mm) and the disc bayonet tabs stand 1.0-1.08 mm proud of their faces, so a lone cube rests on its disc tabs, not on the 0.1 mm silicone tread.

## Flex net (`flex_net.svg`, `flex_net.dxf`)

Six 12.18 mm panels in a cross: column +y, +z, -y, -z, with -x and +x beside +z. The net is 0.11 mm 2-layer flex, drawn as seen from outside. Layers:

1. Outline (black).
1. Fold lines (grey dashed).
1. Windows (red): leg strips, bayonet slit arcs and entry notches, hook slots (14 mm build only), disc-pin sockets, disc windows, 0.4 mm sphere windows.
1. Insert keep-outs (orange).
1. Pads (gold): corner contact rings at the 16 corner-sphere positions (4 per plain panel), 26 SMA crimp pads labelled with their wire, battery tabs BAT+ and BAT- on the -z panel.
1. Footprints (blue): 6 IR LED and 6 photodiode 0402, one pair per panel, as near the centre as the windows allow (2.98-5.93 mm off centre; the centres are under legs or discs); 16 Schottky 0201, two per corner.
1. Stator coils (violet): two folding flaps above the +-x panels, 3 sector coils on 2 layers each, coil outer radius = rotor radius, with stiffener rings.
1. Stiffener outlines (green dashed): the end-plate rings, the coil flaps.
1. Power rails (orange-red): VBAT ring traces along all four plain panels, 0.35 mm from the edges. These are the only routed nets; the signal, gate and coil nets are not routed.

Not placed (no free rectangle on any panel): MCU CH32V003F4U6 (QFN-20 3x3), LIS2DW12 (2x2), TP4054 (SOT-23-5), 6 tantalum 1206, all 19 SOT-523 FETs (13 wire switches plus 6 coil phases). The free area left is 29-39 mm2 per panel, in fragments. Components on the inner side would stand 0.4-0.8 mm into the cavity through skin pockets. The ones that were placed are in the overlap check: the IR parts touch bolts np and pn and a rack pawl by 0.01-0.015 mm3.

## Assembly order (as designed; blocked where the overlap check says so)

1. Press steel inserts into the frame's insert holes. Glue the corner spheres into the carriers, and the ring spheres into the disc bosses (from below).
1. Fit the leg pins, legs and tendon (tied through the knuckle groove) on the frame's hinge bosses.
1. Slide each edge bolt into its corner from an open tube end, then drop its two carriers into the corners against the rails, with the return springs.
1. Fit the rack pawls with their crimped 76 um wires and the unlock loop. Fit the capstans on their 0.25 pins with three pawls each, wind the tendons, and crimp the leg wires.
1. Put the battery into the middle slab. **This is where the build fails: the cell and both joint stacks claim the same volume.**
1. Build each joint into its end plate: sun shaft with the G220 pressed on, planets, worm arbor with GW205 and G220, idler on the selector arm, rotor and pin. Then fit the disc hub over the planets.
1. Close both ends with the end plates and fit the sprung disc pins.
1. Wrap the flex (inner side with the pads against the skin) and solder the crimp pins through the skin holes. Fold the coil flaps in under the rotors before closing the last end.

## Designed in CAD versus sketched

In CAD: all printed parts listed above. Bought parts as envelopes. Wires as straight runs. The flex net as a 2D drawing with the placed components as 3D envelopes.

Sketched only (described, not modelled): the trailing-carrier pull link; the leg lock-pawl lift cam; return springs for bolts and carriers; pawl anchors (pads are free-floating in the assembly); worm arbor and planet bearings; the selector swing arm and its coil; bolt rails beyond the skin corner; tendon guides between knuckle and capstan drum (the tendon must cross the bolt bar on legs XP and XN, through a slot that is not cut); the profile shift of the planets' R2 half; involute tooth forms (all gears are straight-sided trapezoids); a helical worm thread (the bought GW205 is a cylinder).
