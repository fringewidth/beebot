Neural cellular automata · hill-climb

Beebot

Every square of the grid runs the same tiny network and sees only its 3x3 neighborhood. It can only pass mass to its neighbors; mass is never created. There is no smell: a robot only knows the cheese is there when it touches it. The swarm has to search, then pull itself together around the cheese using pheromones it lays on the floor, with no leader and no map.

v1 had a smell that pointed every square at the cheese. It reached 0.267 on its test set against a hand-written baseline's 0.405, then saturated. That version is tagged v1-smell in the repo.

Where it stands

Val score is the share of the rollout spent before the cheese is fully surrounded, averaged over unseen grid sizes, oversized grids and cheeses, and a damaged-swarm test. A failure counts as 1. Lower is better.

Curriculum progress

Training starts with small cheese close by and unlocks harder layouts once the success rate holds above 80%. The final level is cheese of up to 9 squares, up to 20 squares away.

Rules against fooling ourselves

  • Runs are ranked on the hard metric, never on the loss.
  • Val uses grid sizes never trained on, grids and cheeses bigger than any in training, and a test that wipes a quarter of the mass mid-run.
  • Rollout length, firing rate and the occupancy threshold are fixed in the evaluator, so no training setting can make the test easier.
  • A separate test set with other sizes and seeds is run once on the finalists, after the hill-climb.
  • A fraction of a robot can't drive itself: a square moves and writes pheromone in proportion to the mass it holds. The first v2 swarm found a valid strategy under the old rules: spread into a thin fog over the whole world, then condense onto the cheese, like a slime mold. Physical robots can't do that, because the fog gave every square a neighbor to relay signals through. Fog is still measured on every run.
  • Empty floor can't think. Only squares holding robots write pheromones, and pheromones fade 5% per step.
